# Malta's big five banks on your phone: how their apps really score

We scored the apps of BOV, HSBC, APS, BNF and Lombard on onboarding, everyday use, features and wallets. The best reaches 3.8 out of 5; most sit at 2 or below.

## Bank by bank

1. **APS Bank · myAPS** (Best of the five), 3.8 / 5: The most complete of the five. Existing customers can open new accounts in the app, pay by mobile number, change debit card limits and monitor investments, and APS says it was the first local bank to drop online SEPA payment fees. Cards work with Apple Pay and Google Wallet. The gap: a brand new customer still has to become an APS customer and sign up through the website before the app is any use.

2. **Bank of Valletta · BOV Mobile** (Strong features, weak reviews), 3.0 / 5: The feature list is solid: Stop A Card for lost cards, payments to friends by mobile number, top-ups and bill payments, and an in-app link to add cards to Apple Pay. But the rating is the lowest of the apps with a score, and recent reviews describe missing accounts and login trouble. New customers can apply online only for a personal savings account, through a separate website, not the app.

3. **HSBC Malta** (Best rated, least open), 2.0 / 5: Customers rate it highly and it does the basics reliably: instant SEPA payments to new payees, Global Accounts, approving card payments online and managing open banking consents. But it is for existing online banking customers only, bills and third-party payees mostly have to be set up first, and HSBC Malta is not on Apple's list of Apple Pay banks in Malta.

4. **BNF Bank · BNF Mobile App** (Best onboarding), 2.0 / 5: BNF is the only one of the five where a new customer can open an account 24/7 from a phone or tablet with a Maltese ID or residence card, launched in 2024. After that the app is basic: balances, own transfers, quick payments, bills and secure messages. Too few ratings to show a score, recent reviews report crashes, and BNF is not on Apple's Apple Pay list for Malta.

5. **Lombard Bank** (Branch first), 0.5 / 5: Lombard's internet banking runs from its website, and the bank's own FAQ presents its mobile app as the way to get a software token that generates security codes. To sign up for internet banking you are asked to visit a branch. It is a secure, traditional set-up, but there is little here for someone who wants to bank from a phone.

The pattern is clear: Malta's traditional banks have added apps on top of branch processes rather than building around the phone. Only one advertises opening an account end to end from a phone, only two appear on Apple's Apple Pay list for Malta, and none of the five store listings mention in-app spending insights or virtual cards.
