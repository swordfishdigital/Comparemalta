# Lahmacun: what it is, where it comes from and three places to eat it in Malta

A paper-thin flatbread with spiced minced meat, rolled up with parsley and lemon. Its story runs from medieval Aleppo to Gaziantep, and these are three places that serve it in Malta.

## Three regional styles

1. **Antep** (EU protected since 2025), Gaziantep: Thin and crisp, seasoned with garlic. The style most people in Turkey picture first.

2. **Urfa** (Regional style), Şanlıurfa: Onion instead of garlic, often with a spicier pepper paste.

3. **Aleppo style** (Regional style), Halep işi: Sweet and sour from pomegranate molasses, closest to the dish's Levantine roots.

## Where to eat it in Malta

4. **Kebab Factory** (Easiest to find), Msida, St Julian's, Paceville, Valletta, Ħamrun, San Ġwann, Mellieħa: A Turkish chain founded in Malta in 2019, with lahmacun as a core part of a menu built around döner, kebabs, Turkish pizza and baklava. Diners at the Msida and St Julian's branches single out the lahmacun, and with seven branches it is the simplest way to try it wherever you are on the island.

5. **Moo's Kebab** (Diners' favourite), Gżira (The Strand), Valletta: Lahmacun sits in the pide section of the menu next to Turkish-style pizzas. The Gżira branch on The Strand is one of the best reviewed Turkish places on the island, with 4.5 out of 5 from 571 reviews on Tripadvisor, and there is a second branch in Valletta.

6. **Kebab Bistro** (Classic recipe), Sliema (153 Tower Road): A Turkish bistro on Tower Road whose lahmacun follows the classic mix of minced beef, tomato, onion, garlic, parsley and herbs, and is one of the popular items on its delivery menu. Good for a sit-down Turkish meal in Sliema with soups, pides and grills alongside.

Tip: order it with lemon and fresh herbs and eat it straight away, while the base is still crisp. Menus and opening hours change, so check with the restaurant before you go.
