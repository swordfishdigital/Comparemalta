Five Maltese bakeries worth getting up early for

Wood-fired ovens, wartime stories and the Gozitan ftira: five traditional bakeries across Malta and Gozo that still bake the old way.

By CompareMalta.ai

Photos: Bellina 09, CC BY-SA 4.0, Wikimedia Commons

Maltese bread is a daily ritual: a crusty ħobża for the table, a ring-shaped ftira for ħobż biż-żejt, and in Gozo a ftira that is closer to a pizza. Many village bakeries still fire their ovens with wood, which gives the loaves their uneven shape and thick crust.

There is no official ranking of bakeries, so this is not a league table. We picked five places that independent food writers keep coming back to and ordered them as a day out: three in Malta, then two in Nadur, Gozo.

Our five

1

Falzon Bakery

100+ yearsRabat · Ħobża, ftira, buns

On a side street near St Paul's Church, in a 17th century building that has been a bakery for more than a century. A well inside still supplies the water for the dough, and in the Second World War it was the only place handing out bread in this hard-hit area. Come for a warm ħobża and stay for the buns.

Photos: Joseolgon, CC BY 4.0, Wikimedia Commons
2

Rojolin Bakery

Wood-firedBalzan · Ftajjar, ħobż tal-Malti

A family bakery open since the Second World War, run by Anton Micallef, his wife Romina and her sister Lina. They bake hundreds of ftajjar and Maltese loaves a day with no additives or preservatives, and swear by the wood oven for the irregular shape and a crust that stays right.

Map ↗
Photos: EnriqueTabone, CC BY-SA 4.0, Wikimedia Commons
3

Emanuel's Bakery

Bread townQormi · Bread, imqaret

Qormi is Malta's historic bread town, with close to fifty bakeries, and Emanuel's is a name food writers single out, including for imqaret, the fried date pastries. Time a visit for October, when the town celebrates its bakers with the Lejl f'Casal Fornaro festival.

Map ↗
Photos: Continentaleurope, CC BY-SA 3.0, Wikimedia Commons
4

Mekren's Bakery

Gozitan ftiraNadur, Gozo · Ftira Għawdxija

A decades-old bakery and one of the two homes of the Gozitan ftira: bread dough topped with thin potato slices, onion and tomato, plus olives, capers and tuna or anchovies if you want it traditional. Order at the hole in the wall and wait outside while it bakes, or take one half-baked to finish at home.

Map ↗
Photos: Andrew Milligan sumo, CC BY 2.0, Wikimedia Commons
5

Maxokk Bakery

Gozitan ftiraNadur, Gozo · Ftira Għawdxija

Mekren's friendly rival, a short walk away on St James Street. Same hole-in-the-wall ritual, same pride in a ftira baked in a baker's oven. Locals have their favourite of the two; the only fair test is to try both on the same day.
